Hcp::Job::Appointment

Appointments

One visit a job is booked for, where the work takes more than a single trip.

An appointment is reached through its job, either as a relation of its own or brought back beside a whole page of jobs at once. It reads six things and it writes nothing.

A job is booked once and visited as often as the work needs. The booking is the job's schedule; each trip is an appointment.

Two ways to an appointment

#

An appointment is not read from the top. Housecall Pro keeps it under the job it belongs to, so Hcp::Job::Appointment has no all, no where and no find of its own — it is reached through a job, and there are two ways there.

One request per job. job.appointments is a relation over the list Housecall Pro keeps at jobs/<id>/appointments. It reads nothing until it is walked, and then reads the whole list in one answer:

job = Hcp::Job.find 'job_77cbcb51acb1442fa9554131d7d1f543'

job.appointments.count       # => 1
job.appointments.first       # => the first visit
job.appointments.to_a        # => every visit the job is booked for
job.appointments.find 'appt_02aad0c5d573454cae36e5fa24c839bc'

One request for a page of jobs. includes(:appointments) asks Housecall Pro to send the visits beside each job, and they read off the job's schedule — twenty jobs and their visits for the price of one request:

Hcp::Job.includes(:appointments).limit(20).flat_map { |job| job.schedule.appointments }

Ask for nothing and nothing arrives. A job read without the expansion carries an empty list inside its schedule rather than a missing one:

Hcp::Job.first.schedule.appointments # => []

An estimate carries a schedule too, and reads its visits the same way.

What an appointment reads

# 
appointment = job.appointments.first

appointment.id             # => 'appt_02aad0c5d573454cae36e5fa24c839bc'
appointment.starts_at      # => 2026-06-26 10:45:00 UTC
appointment.ends_at        # => 2026-06-26 12:45:00 UTC
appointment.arrival_window # => 30, minutes
appointment.anytime?       # => false
appointment.dispatched_employee_ids
# => ['pro_738721e93c12463ea2e756267f5a5fe7']
Hcp::Job::Appointment
ReaderReads
idthe ID Housecall Pro files the visit under
starts_atwhen the visit is booked to start, or nil
ends_atwhen the visit is booked to end, or nil
arrival_windowhow many minutes wide the arrival window is
anytime?whether the visit is booked for a day rather than for a time
dispatched_employee_idsthe IDs of the pros sent on the visit

anytime? is what tells a booked day from a booked time. Where it is true, the customer was given a date and no hour, and arrival_window is beside the point.

A visit carries IDs for the pros rather than the pros themselves. Housecall Pro sends no names here, so the gem reads none — the pros a whole job is assigned to read as employees, off job.assigned_employees.

A schedule is not an appointment

#

A job has one schedule and any number of visits. The schedule is the booking the customer was given — one window, one time zone. The visits are the trips it takes to do the work:

job.schedule.starts_at # => 2026-06-26 10:45:00 UTC, the booking
job.schedule.time_zone # => 'America/New_York'

job.appointments.map &:starts_at
# => [2026-06-26 10:45:00 UTC, 2026-07-02 09:00:00 UTC]

Most jobs are one trip, and then the two say the same thing. The schedule is on the records page.

What an appointment does not do

#

Booking a visit, moving one and canceling one all happen in Housecall Pro. This gem takes no write for an appointment, so Hcp::Job::Appointment has no create, no update and no destroy.

Housecall Pro takes no conditions on a job's appointments either, so the relation walks, counts, limits and finds by ID, and refuses nothing because there is nothing to refuse — where, order and includes are simply not there.
