Hcp::Lead

Leads

Somebody who asked a Housecall Pro user for work, before there is a job.

A lead is opened with a name, a number and an address, and moved through the account's own pipeline by the name of a status. These are the only two methods in the gem that write.

A lead carries its own key

#

Leads were here before the key became global, and they still take one. Hcp::Lead and Hcp::Lead::Pipeline are handed a key: and a company_id: when they are built, and neither reads Hcp.key. Everything else in the gem does the opposite: one key, set once, and a location per call.

lead = Hcp::Lead.new key: ENV['HCP_KEY'], company_id: company_id

Both arguments are required, and neither has a default, so a process serving several locations names the one it means every time.

Opening a lead

#

create posts the lead to Housecall Pro and keeps what it filed the lead and the customer under:

lead = Hcp::Lead.new key: ENV['HCP_KEY'], company_id: company_id

lead.create name: 'Ada', phone: '5550000001', email: 'ada@example.com',
  address: { street: '1 Example Street', city: 'Springfield', state: 'CA', zip: '90210' },
  note: 'Very interested in buying', source: 'The Lead Generator'

lead.id          # => 'lea_9f0a1c8d2b4e4f0f8c1d2e3f4a5b6c7d'
lead.customer_id # => 'cus_c3fd9f15d014442aa3165fccf68ae95c'

Six keys, and every one of them optional. What Housecall Pro calls each of them is not what a caller would:

What create takes
KeyWhere it landsWhat it is
:namecustomer.first_namewhoever asked for the work
:emailcustomer.emailthe address to answer them at
:phonecustomer.mobile_numberthe number to reach them on
:sourcelead_source, on the lead and on the customerwhere the lead came from
:addressaddressa hash: street, city, state, zip
:notenotewhatever the lead should say

Anything left blank is left out of the payload rather than sent as an empty string, so a lead with no email opens with no email rather than with one Housecall Pro would file as blank.

A lead that Housecall Pro will not open raises Hcp::Error, carrying what it said. A connection it will not take raises the same, so one rescue covers both:

begin
  lead.create name: 'Ada'
rescue Hcp::Error => error
  error.message # => what Housecall Pro said, or what the connection said
end

A lead already opened

#

Where the lead is already on the account, build one around the IDs rather than opening another:

lead = Hcp::Lead.new id: 'lea_9f0a1c8d2b4e4f0f8c1d2e3f4a5b6c7d',
  customer_id: 'cus_c3fd9f15d014442aa3165fccf68ae95c',
  key: ENV['HCP_KEY'], company_id: company_id

lead.id          # => 'lea_9f0a1c8d2b4e4f0f8c1d2e3f4a5b6c7d'
lead.customer_id # => 'cus_c3fd9f15d014442aa3165fccf68ae95c'

id and customer_id are the whole of what a lead reads. There is no Hcp::Lead.find and no Hcp::Lead.all: this gem speaks two lead endpoints, a POST and the pipeline's PUT, and nothing that reads one back. What a lead was turned into arrives instead as a webhook.

Moving a lead through the pipeline

#

The pipeline is where a Housecall Pro user files a lead while they are still chasing it. update moves the lead to the status going by a name:

pipeline = Hcp::Lead::Pipeline.new id: lead.id,
  key: ENV['HCP_KEY'], company_id: company_id

pipeline.update status_name: 'Won'

The name is the account's own word for the status, not a word this gem has a list of — an account that calls it 'Closed — sold' is asked for exactly that. The gem reads the account's statuses, finds the one going by the name, and refuses where there is none rather than moving the lead somewhere unintended:

pipeline.update status_name: 'Nowhere'
# => Hcp::Error: Status Nowhere not found for lead lea_9f0a1c8d2b4e4f0f8c1d2e3f4a5b6c7d

That is two requests: one to read the statuses, one to move the lead. A refusal from either raises Hcp::Error carrying what Housecall Pro said.

The only two writes

#

Hcp::Lead#create and Hcp::Lead::Pipeline#update are the only methods in this gem that change anything on a Housecall Pro account. Jobs, customers, estimates, appointments and every record under them read, and only read.

Neither class inherits from Hcp::Resource, which since 1.3.0 is a record rather than a holder of credentials. That is the one breaking change in the release, and nothing documented ever called what it moved.
